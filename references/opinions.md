# Opinions — what Robert actually thinks

**One opinion per piece, maximum, and it should carry a number or a mechanism.** A position with evidence reads as confidence. Three in a row reads as a rant. A position with nothing behind it reads as marketing.

Everything below came from Robert directly, or is a policy the business demonstrably operates. Nothing here is invented to sound good.

---

## 1. Cheap rates buy you a worn-out car

**The take, in his words:**

> "A lot of companies try to undercut the market in Miami, but oftentimes the cars are not as well kept because of the sheer volume they push. We focus on delivery and experience that actually makes you feel like that car is yours, and not a clapped out Lamborghini you got a good deal on."

**The mechanism:** cheap rates require volume, volume wears cars out, and the thing you came for stops being worth buying. The discount is real; it's just funded by the condition of the car.

**Why it's the strongest opinion in the file:** it has a causal argument, not just a complaint. It reframes a price objection into a condition question, and it lets us talk about competitors' pricing without ever naming one.

**The limit:** only usable while our own cars are genuinely kept sharp. It's an argument that can be checked at handover.

---

## 2. Who we say no to is part of what you're buying

Lussaro refuses underqualified renters, drivers without insurance, and applicants who don't pass screening.

Most operators can't afford to be selective. Saying it out loud is a flex and a truth simultaneously — the person renting after you was screened the same way you were, and the car reflects it.

**How to write it:** *"we screen every renter."* Do not enumerate what disqualifies someone. Blanket criminal-record exclusions are a legally sensitive thing to publish, and the vaguer phrasing is both safer and reads as more premium.

**The evidence behind it:** the first Urus rental. See `stories.md`. This position was bought and paid for.

---

## 3. The smart first exotic isn't the flashiest one

Robert's first car was a Maybach GLS 600, not a Lamborghini — *"an affordable exotic, so to speak,"* chosen for its cap rate.

**Why it's worth publishing:** it's a genuinely unusual admission in this industry, and it's the reason we can recommend the cheaper car without ego. We're thinking about the fleet, not about which car flatters us. Customers benefit from exactly the same reasoning when we point them at the S580.

---

## 4. Recommend the cheaper car

Under price pressure the reflex is to ask the budget and offer the S580 at $445 — *"it's just as nice as the Maybach."*

**The number:** $445 against $895, and the S580 is quicker to 60 than the Cullinan.

**Why it matters:** it's the rarest behavior in the industry and it's completely genuine here. It also protects the customer from feeling they've traded down, which is the actual skill in it.

---

## 5. Insurance costs what it costs

Full coverage on a Lamborghini for a driver with no policy of their own is expensive — up to $499 a day on the Urus, from $99 depending on the car and the driver's age.

**The position:** it's a pass-through, not a margin line, and it's the one number we don't move on. We'll flex on our own rate to close a deal. We won't pretend the risk is cheaper than it is.

**Why it's publishable:** it converts what looks like a hidden fee into a stated principle. The number is large enough that a customer discovering it late is a genuine problem — which is exactly why it should be early and explained.

---

## 6. Mileage should be pooled, not capped daily

100 miles a day, added across the whole booking. Three days is 300 miles, spendable however you like, and only the total at return counts.

**The number:** a daily cap that resets at midnight is a materially worse deal at the same headline price. Drive to Fort Lauderdale on day one and you're over, even if the car sat still the other two days.

---

## 7. Say the expensive true thing

The delivery fee on this site was published as one charge per rental. It's actually one way — collection is charged again — so the real cost of a round trip was being understated by roughly half. It was corrected upward, on eight pages, and now reads from a single file so it can't drift back.

**The number:** $125 each way in Miami. A round trip is about $250, not $125.

**Why it works:** correcting a price *upward*, unprompted, is the one move a dishonest operator will never make. Told plainly, without congratulating ourselves, it's more persuasive than any claim about service.

## Positions we do NOT take

- **"We never negotiate."** Untrue — Robert will move to close a deal. The honest version is that the published price is the same starting number for everyone. See `stats.md`.
- **"No hidden fees."** Too broad while insurance and the chauffeur minimum aren't published. Once they are, this becomes available.
- **Anything comparing us to a named competitor.**
- **Anything about fleet size, ranking, or how long we've been operating.** See the hard rules in `voice.md`.
